## In short

Three changes to the lossy DLL's rules. Slow fades keep up much better, small dark things that move without motion vectors are shown more
fully when the camera is still, and less of a moving object's old image is left beside it. Flat areas at rest and moving backgrounds
change a little more per frame, still less than with AMD's shaders. Up to 0.02 ms more per frame at 4K.

| | AMD's shaders | `dll-2026-10-13.2` | This release |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brightening fade: frames behind | 0.46 | 0.87 | **0.64** |
| Dark leaves shown, camera still | 79.1% | 75.5% | **77.5%** |
| Still object over fast ground: error where its old image lands | 1.62 | 2.64 | **2.26** |
| Flat areas at rest: change per frame (worse) | 0.126 | 0.057 | 0.078 |
| Whole upscaler, 4K, average per frame | 4.87 ms | 2.21 ms | 2.24 ms |

Errors are against the true image, in steps of 255; lower is better. Frames that skip the network unless stated; auto exposure off
unless stated.

## Change 1: a change has to last two frames

With the camera still, a skipped frame takes nothing from the new frame unless a test finds that the content changed. The previous
release lowered that test's threshold for small dark things, which halved the pulse of dark leaves falling in front of a dark sky. In a
single frame it still cannot tell the edge of a leaf from film grain.

Now every frame notes, per pixel, whether the new frame lies above or below the old picture by more than 0.02. A skipped frame counts
the content as changed where its own difference passes that amount in the same direction as the note of the frame before. Grain changes
in a new place each frame. A leaf that has moved in, or the sky where a leaf has just been, differs on two frames in a row. The note
travels through the first shader, which reads that image anyway, so it costs nothing measurable.

| Dark leaves, skipped frames; auto exposure off / on | AMD's shaders | `dll-2026-10-13.2` | This release |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camera still: share of the leaf's darkness shown | 79.1 / 80.9% | 75.5 / 75.9% | **77.5 / 77.4%** |
| Camera still: left where a leaf was up to 3 frames ago | 5.4 / 5.1% | 8.4 / 9.0% | **7.9 / 8.3%** |
| Slow pan: shown | 69.3 / 70.1% | 70.1 / 71.8% | 70.0 / 71.6% |
| Fast pan: shown | 72.9 / 74.6% | 60.8 / 62.1% | 60.6 / 62.0% |
| Tiny slow particles: left behind | 10.5 / 10.5% | 12.8 / 13.4% | **11.5 / 11.7%** |

## Change 2: a tighter limit beside moving objects

On a skipped frame, beside anything that moves differently from the pixel, the last shader limits the old picture to the range of the
nine new samples around the pixel. That range used to be widened by half of itself. Since `dll-2026-10-13.2` the limit applies only
beside different motion, which made the tight form affordable: less of an object's old image is let through.

| Error where a thing's old image would land; auto exposure off / on | AMD's shaders | `dll-2026-10-13.2` | This release |
|---|---|---|---|
| Object moving in front of a still camera (8,0): its outline | 1.07 / 1.07 | 1.30 / 1.33 | **1.15 / 1.17** |
| Still bright object over fast ground, same depth | 1.62 / 1.61 | 2.64 / 2.43 | **2.26 / 2.20** |
| Thin bars fixed on screen in front of a pan, mean of four pans | 0.82 | 0.91 | **0.86** |
| Just-uncovered areas, panning scene, 5K output (dB, higher is better) | 39.90 / 40.51 | 39.90 / 40.65 | **40.34 / 41.12** |

The still object over fast ground is the picture at the top of this page.

## Change 3: slow fades

When a scene grows lighter or darker with nothing moving, bright flat areas followed up to half a frame later than with AMD's shaders.
The lag sat on the frames that skip the network: three of their rules, all there to keep the picture steady at rest, held a pixel back
(a change too small to matter is dropped, nothing is taken from the new frame where nothing moves, and the new frame's share is squared).

Now the note of change 1 also records whether the green channel drifted up or down by a small amount, and it reaches every frame. Where
the same small drift repeats, the small change is no longer dropped, and on a skipped frame the pixel takes the network's own weight.
Only drifts up to 1% of the brightness count, which keeps film grain and fine texture out.

| Bright flat areas during a slow fade (1% a frame), frames behind; auto exposure off / on | AMD's shaders | `dll-2026-10-13.2` | This release |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brightening | 0.46 / 0.46 | 0.87 / 0.91 | **0.64 / 0.71** |
| Darkening | 0.62 / 0.72 | 0.82 / 1.11 | **0.68 / 0.81** |
| Mid-bright flat areas, brightening | 0.89 / 0.77 | 1.22 / 1.10 | **0.91 / 0.82** |
| Mid-bright flat areas, darkening | 0.91 / 0.90 | 0.97 / 1.04 | **0.78 / 0.86** |

## Unchanged, speed and checks

- **Within 2% or the usual scatter:** fine detail at rest, the still picture, the moving scene's accuracy at every size and from 30 to
  120 FPS, the other ghost and plate scenes, wires, fast sparks, rain, fireflies, glints and swaying foliage.
- **Changes 1 and 2 do not interact:** every row that moved, moved as with one of them alone, give or take 0.01. Change 3 was checked on
  top of them.
- **Exact DLL:** the same file as before.
- **DLL = Linux files:** 48 of 48 test-rig comparisons identical.
