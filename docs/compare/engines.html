Two Lite engines, one rhythm apart

The two FSR 4.1.2 Lite builds of this project, drawn as glass engines. One turn of the crank is one game frame. Each frame enters at the intake on the left, goes through the prepass, the network's three passes (the cylinders) and the postpass, and leaves as the upscaled picture on the right; the postpass also sends a copy back as history for the next frame. The two engines turn together; drag one to look around it, and zoom with + and −, a pinch, or Ctrl and the mouse wheel. The picture screens always face you.

Same picture as AMD's Lite

4.1.2l-exact

All three cylinders fire on every turn, as in AMD's own Lite. The red outline is AMD's engine running alongside: it never separates, because the output is the same, byte for byte. The engine is machined finer, so each turn costs less.

  • Intake turbine: the prepass gathers the frame's color, depth and motion
  • Cylinders 1-3: the network's three passes, AMD's weights
  • Output rotor: the postpass builds the picture
  • Return pipe: history for the next frame
  • Red outline: AMD's Lite, for comparison
4-17%less time per frame than AMD's Lite, Windows timing-kit reports

Changes the picture

4.1.2l-lossy

The cylinders fire on every other turn. On the turns between, the clutch opens and the network rests: the tank holds the last network output, and the gimbal moves it along the game's motion before the postpass uses it. Its weights were retrained for this rhythm, so the crank turns faster.

  • Intake turbine: the prepass, every frame
  • Cylinders 1-3: the network, every other frame, retrained weights
  • Clutch: opens on the frames the network skips
  • Tank and gimbal: the stored output, moved with the motion
  • Output rotor: the postpass, every frame
12-25%less time per frame than AMD's Lite, Windows timing-kit reports
