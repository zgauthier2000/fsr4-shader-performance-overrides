Cyboman Computer Music

The songs of the FSR 4 timing kit: fourteen tracks, about 25 minutes in all, about the project itself. They are written as code. Every note, drum hit and instrument comes from a Python script, and the singing comes from a text-to-speech voice taught to hold a tune.

What it is

The FSR 4 performance overrides project ships a free timing kit. People run it on their own graphics cards and send in the results. A benchmark takes a few minutes, so the kit has a music player: press Space to play or pause, and the arrow keys to skip. While a song plays, the line being sung shows at the top of the window.

Everything it plays is Cyboman Computer Music. The songs are jokes and stories from the project itself: exact builds, skipped frames, a shader cache that lied, a drive that had to be reseated, and a thank-you to the testers. New songs reach the kit as "song packs" through the same update channel as its test builds.

Written as codeWords, melody, chords and arrangement live in a Python script per song, written by cyboman with Claude (an AI assistant).
Instruments from mathNo samples and no music software. Each sound is computed in numpy from simple waves, noise and a plucked-string model.
A voice that singsA text-to-speech voice speaks each word, then a singing step sets the pitch and length onto the melody's notes.

How a song is made

The instruments

The early songs use the palette of 1980s game consoles: pulse waves, a triangle and noise. Later songs add a plucked string, saw-wave pads, a fiddle-like lead and a wooden flute, all built from the same kind of math. Teaching a speaking voice to sing

Text-to-speech voices talk; they don't sing. So each word is spoken on its own, and then its pitch is held onto the melody's note and its vowel is stretched to the note's length, keeping the consonants crisp. A little vibrato makes the held notes sound sung rather than stuck.

Songs 01 to 11 use Piper's "alba" voice. Songs 12 to 14 use Qwen3-TTS, a newer, more human-sounding voice, with Seed-VC smoothing the result. In "Thank You" the voice is never pitch-shifted at all: it is spoken word, placed in time over synthesized weather.

Checks before a song ships

  • Can you understand the words? Whisper, a speech-recognition model, transcribes the finished mix and the result is compared with the lyrics. The newer songs reach about 90 to 95% of words recognized. Low scores mean the voice is changed and the song rendered again.
  • Same loudness: every song is leveled to -16 LUFS (EBU R128), so skipping between them never jumps in volume.
  • Lyrics and credits travel inside the file: each Ogg file carries its timed lyric lines and its credits in its tags, so a song pack needs nothing else.
  • Picked by ear: many songs and versions were made and dropped. Only the ones cyboman liked after listening went into a pack.
