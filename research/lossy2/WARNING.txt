WARNING: THIS VERSION CHANGES THE PICTURE, MORE THAN THE LOSSY VERSION DOES.

It is NOT the same as the "exact" version of this project, NOT the same as AMD's file, and NOT the same as
the "lossy" version beside it.

- The exact version produces exactly AMD's picture, byte for byte. This one does not.
  If you want AMD's picture, use the exact version.
- lossy2 is an experiment next to lossy, not its replacement. If you are happy with lossy, you can keep it.

What it is
- Everything the lossy version does (the network runs on every other frame, the frames in between reuse
  its last result moved along with the picture's motion; see lossy's WARNING.txt), plus: six of the network's
  twelve layers carry new weights. They were trained on this machine to match AMD's picture through the whole
  lossy frame loop, starting from lossy's own weights. The other six layers are AMD's.
- The new weights are used in the Quality, Balanced and Performance modes at every output size.
  In Ultra Performance it behaves exactly like the lossy version.
- It is as fast as the lossy version: only weight values inside the same shaders change.

What it does better than lossy (test scenes, 4K Balanced, unless noted)
- Swaying foliage: much closer to AMD's picture (error 12.2 against lossy's 17.9; AMD's own file 12.1).
- Strips that a moving object has just uncovered: about 1 dB more accurate (also at 1080p and 5K).
- Fewer fireflies (single over-bright pixels): about 15 to 30% fewer than lossy.
- Thin wires and fine stripes in motion: a little steadier.

What it costs
- Brightening is slower: when a part of the picture gets brighter (a light switching on, stepping from shade
  into sun), lossy2 takes about twice as many frames as lossy to catch up (1.5 frames against 0.6).
- A still picture is about 0.3 to 0.4 dB less accurate than lossy's (about 0.4 dB below AMD's), at 1080p, 4K and 5K.
- Heavy film grain with the game's auto exposure on changes more from frame to frame than with lossy
  (about 2.7 times as much in the test scene, and more than with AMD's file). With auto exposure off the
  difference is small.
- When a large area changes brightness in one step, the first frame after the step misses a little more of it
  than lossy does.
- Fading out is a little slower than with lossy.
- The weights were trained on 4K Balanced material only; other sizes and modes were checked at 1080p and 5K
  (Balanced), not in every combination.

These figures come from synthetic test scenes on Linux (Proton), not from games. One trained copy is shipped;
training the same way again gave copies that differ (one of three grew bright specks in rain, so it was not used).
